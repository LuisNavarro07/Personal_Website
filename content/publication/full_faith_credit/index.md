abstract: >-
  The conventional view of municipal finance holds that the general obligation (GO) pledge of a
  government's full faith, credit, and taxing power provides a broader and more secure source of
  repayment than an irrevocable, unlimited pledge of enterprise revenues. Yet GO bond issuance has
  declined, with recent data showing that GO debt accounts for about one-third of annual bond
  issuance. We reassess the value of the full faith and credit pledge by examining whether the
  institutional and legal protections embedded in revenue bonds mitigate the risks associated with
  their more limited repayment pledge. While initial findings show a risk premium associated with
  revenue bonds, additional analyses reveal substantial variation across issuers, credit ratings, and
  sectors. Most notably, among highly rated general-purpose governments, we find little evidence of
  meaningful borrowing-cost advantage from pledging the government's full faith, credit, and taxing
  power. These findings suggest that governments must weigh the cost of debt against the benefits of
  preserving budgetary flexibility and limiting demands on their taxing capacity.
