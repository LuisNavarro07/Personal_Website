abstract: >-
  This study examines differences in credit ratings and borrowing costs between State Revolving Fund
  collateralized debt obligations (SRF CDOs) and traditional municipal revenue (REV) and general
  obligation (GO) bonds. The SRF program is an intergovernmental system that finances clean and safe
  drinking water and wastewater infrastructure, and it has deployed a municipal CDO instrument that
  channels hundreds of billions of dollars to state and local governments. Using inverse probability
  weights based on each bond's propensity to be issued as an SRF CDO, we estimate credit rating and
  primary market spread models. SRF status raises the probability of a AAA rating by 38 to 49
  percentage points and shifts the entire rating distribution upward. Holding the rating fixed, the
  securedness of the SRF structure lowers borrowing costs by 15 to 29 basis points, an advantage
  attenuated by fund leverage. The findings indicate that the SRF structure functions as a credit
  enhancement that investors price beyond the formal rating.
