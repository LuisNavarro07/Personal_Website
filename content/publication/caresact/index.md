abstract: >-
  Access to cash shapes how local governments respond to crises. Federal aid can supply liquidity
  directly, but its effectiveness depends on whether it complements or substitutes for local
  borrowing. Using a regression discontinuity design at the population threshold that determined
  eligibility, this paper studies the Coronavirus Relief Fund (CRF). Eligible governments paid spreads
  at issue 39 basis points lower, about 60% of non-eligible governments' average spread, and appear to
  have issued debt less often, suggesting aid substituted for borrowing. Different CRF and Municipal
  Liquidity Facility thresholds for cities point to direct aid, not the Federal Reserve's backstop, as
  the driver.
