## Course Description

Statistical evidence figures prominently in many policy debates. This course aims to make every student a *sophisticated skeptic*: someone who knows the right questions to ask about a statistical analysis in order to judge its validity and relevance, and, for those so inclined, who has the technical skills to produce convincing evidence, not just consume it.

PUBPOL 528 is the second course in a two-course sequence. Building on the statistics and probability foundation of PUBPOL 527, it covers applied statistical modeling and the regression framework: how to choose the right technique for a question, how to estimate and interpret causal effects, and how to critically evaluate real-world policy research. The course uses real and illustrative data and applies concepts through statistical programming in Stata and in-class discussion of published research.

## Course Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

1. **Formulate a research question as a statistical model:** translate a policy question into a regression framework and identify the outcome, explanatory variables, and estimand of interest.
2. **Estimate and interpret OLS regression coefficients:** bivariate and multiple regression; slopes, intercepts, and goodness of fit in applied policy contexts.
3. **Assess the validity of the classical assumptions:** diagnose omitted variable bias, heteroskedasticity, and measurement error, and understand their consequences for estimation and inference.
4. **Conduct and interpret hypothesis tests:** t-tests, F-tests, and confidence intervals; distinguish statistical from practical significance.
5. **Use qualitative information in regression models:** dummy variables and interaction terms.
6. **Select appropriate model specifications:** linear, log, and quadratic functional forms; the consequences of omitting relevant or including irrelevant variables.
7. **Understand and apply methods for causal inference:** the potential outcomes framework, randomized controlled trials, panel data with fixed effects, difference-in-differences, instrumental variables, and regression discontinuity designs.
8. **Critically evaluate published empirical research:** assess internal and external validity, identify threats to causal interpretation, and communicate statistical findings clearly for policy audiences.
