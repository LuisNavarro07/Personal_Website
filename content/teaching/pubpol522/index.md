## Course Description

This course introduces the core tools of financial management for public and nonprofit organizations: how they raise and spend money, how to read and analyze their financial statements, what their programs and services cost, and how budgets are built and used to make decisions. Class sessions combine short lectures, worked problems from the text, and cases that show how these techniques inform real financial strategy. Quiz sections focus on the spreadsheet skills behind the course's three core analyses: a program budget, a cash flow forecast, and a financial statement analysis.

## Course Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

1. Identify, compare, and contrast the revenue and expense/expenditure structures of the different types of public sector organizations.
2. Read and prepare the basic financial statements for nonprofits and governments; evaluate an organization's financial health and the most pressing threats to its long-term financial position; and develop strategies that improve its operating performance.
3. Determine what public sector programs and services cost by applying the principles of cost analysis, cost allocation, and cost-based decision-making, including break-even and other differential cost analyses.
4. Prepare the essential financial management spreadsheets for a public organization (financial ratio analysis, basic financial statements, and program and cash flow budgets) and use them to inform decisions about program design, personnel management, resource allocation, policy development, and financial strategy.
5. Understand the key institutions, processes, incentives, and politics that shape how public organizations allocate their scarce financial resources.
