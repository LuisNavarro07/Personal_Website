## Course Description

Public budgeting is at the heart of governance. Every decision a government makes about what to fund, what to cut, and how to pay for public services is, at its core, a budgetary decision. This course provides an introductory and systematic foundation in the theory and practice of public budgeting and fiscal administration across federal, state, and local governments.

The course is organized around two questions: *How do governments decide how to spend public resources?* and *How are those decisions shaped by political, legal, and institutional constraints?* We move from the economic rationale for government activity and fiscal federalism, through theories of budgetary decision-making and hands-on budget modelling, to budget classification and formats, and finally to federal debt and long-run fiscal sustainability. No formal prerequisites are required.

## Course Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

1. **Explain the rationale for public finance and budgeting:** why governments engage in budgetary activity, how public finance differs from private finance, and what the core functions of government are.
2. **Read and interpret a government budget document:** navigate revenues, expenditures, surpluses, and deficits, and understand how administrative, economic, and functional classifications convey different information about priorities and accountability.
3. **Describe and evaluate the budget process at the federal, state, and local levels:** trace the four phases of the budget cycle and compare how state and local processes differ under balanced-budget requirements and open-economy constraints.
4. **Analyze fiscal federalism and intergovernmental fiscal relations:** the assignment of fiscal responsibilities, the mechanics and incentive effects of grants, and fiscal disparities and their equity implications.
5. **Evaluate major budget reform systems:** compare budget formats with reference to the service delivery chain of inputs, outputs, and outcomes.
6. **Apply practical methods of cost estimation and revenue forecasting:** growth rates, deterministic expenditure projections, and elementary forecasting techniques.
7. **Assess federal debt and long-run fiscal sustainability:** deficits, debt, and the debt ceiling; the dynamics of debt accumulation; fiscal rules; and long-run pressures, including entitlements and generational equity.
